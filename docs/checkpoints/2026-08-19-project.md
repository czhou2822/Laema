## Combat

### Current decisions

- **Decision:** Combat has exactly two pillars: **Decomposition** and **Reintegration**. This replaces the earlier three-pillar constraint.
- **Decision:** **Transformative mastery** is the overarching outcome produced jointly by both pillars.
- **Decision — Decomposition:** closely examine an initially overwhelming problem, then recursively narrow its scope until reaching a minimum executable and observable result.
- **Decision — stopping rule:** “minimum” means the smallest result that provides meaningful, observable evidence about the larger problem, not the smallest possible action.
- **Decision — Comprehension:** the player consciously owns the isolated result: they can **anticipate** it, **explain** its complete flow, and **extend** it by knowing where to look or act next.

### Current working structure

The following six bullets are a **Proposal** for three peer aspects under each pillar. They are not yet fully settled:

#### Decomposition

- **Comprehension** — the player consciously owns the isolated result; anticipate, explain, and extend are its current facets.
- **Divide and conquer** — the player separates the whole challenge into smaller, manageable parts.
- **Exploratory practice** — the player chooses a focus, tests different approaches, and deliberately improves without one prescribed method.

#### Reintegration

- **Fluency** — practised responses become natural, reliable, and require less conscious effort.
- **Integration** — separately mastered parts reconnect into one continuous performance.
- **Adaptation under pressure** — the player applies and recombines familiar learning during uninterrupted or unfamiliar challenges.

Both pillars contribute to **Transformative mastery**; it is not a seventh bullet.

- **Rejected:** using **Anticipate, Explain, Extend** as the complete three-part definition of Decomposition. They remain the accepted facets of Comprehension, but do not by themselves describe recursive narrowing.
- **Rejected:** the assistant-generated trio **Tractability, Verifiability, Comprehension**. It was supplied when the user asked to identify missing pieces and was rejected because Socrates should guide discovery rather than provide an unsolicited taxonomy.

## Narrative Room

### Reference facts and canon boundaries discussed

- **Fact:** The story is framed by *Heroes of Might and Magic III* continuity. *The Shadow of Death* follows the first defeat of Sandro; its four principal heroes then separate, while Sandro survives and begins the second plan that leads toward *Restoration of Erathia*.
- **Fact:** The canonical murder chain discussed was Sandro’s poison scheme, Lord Haart’s access to King Nicolas Gryphonheart, and the coordinated Nighon–Eeofol invasion. The exact poison composition, preparation details, discovery of the body, and early suspicions are not established by the original sources.
- **Fact:** Cloudfire is a Tower/Wizard outpost in Erathia. The original opening establishes that Wizards were prepared but overrun by Nighon’s Dungeon forces; Catherine reaches the aftermath after the invasion is already underway.
- **Fact:** Canon does not explain Cloudfire’s owner, founder, political relationship to Bracada, or why Wizards were stationed there. The earlier claim that the opening CG definitely showed later Bracadan reinforcements was unsupported and withdrawn.
- **Fact:** Bracada is an independent kingdom and its confirmed formal aid comes after Catherine lands and seeks support; the precise identity of the Tower forces shown in the CG remains **Open**.
- **Constraint:** Lore references and the supplied retrospective are source material, not automatic approval of a pitch, historical window, or new canon.

### Current narrative decisions

- **Decision:** Laema is an original character with no royal-court connection.
- **Decision:** Laema has never met King Nicolas Gryphonheart and will not meet him during the game.
- **Decision:** Laema has no personal connection to the king or his court; widespread news lets her know that he was murdered and poisoned.
- **Decision:** The poison is developed and tested near Laema’s town. Laema has seen its distinctive symptoms in earlier victims and recognizes them when news of the king’s death spreads.
- **Decision:** Laema begins after Sandro’s first defeat, when the four heroes have separated.
- **Rejected:** Laema’s journey intersecting Gem, Gelu, Crag Hack, or Yog.
- **Decision:** The game includes a buildable town that Laema helps develop across the campaign. It is the growing RPG hub and is distinct from Steadwick.
- **Decision:** Steadwick is not the player-built town. It is already an established Erathian capital, so building it from a small settlement was rejected as inconsistent with the available canon.
- **Constraint:** The buildable town is geographically close to Cloudfire and readily accessible during normal play.
- **Decision:** Cloudfire is not the buildable town, but it is a prominent recurring location whose people, routines, and places become familiar before its destruction.
- **Constraint:** Prefer an existing canonical hero as antagonist only if one fits naturally; do not force canon, and use an original character if necessary. The antagonist remains unidentified.

### Current late-game sequence

The earlier direct handoff from Cloudfire’s final blow into the *Restoration of Erathia* opening CG was reopened and is no longer the current design.

1. Laema is present when Nighon’s organized invasion reaches Cloudfire.
2. Laema and Cloudfire’s defenders make their stand and lose; Cloudfire falls.
3. The retreat is sudden and chaotic. Laema has no time to understand the larger situation, choose a cause, or form a plan.
4. Survivors bring the invasion back toward the buildable town.
5. The final battle occurs in the buildable town, before Catherine reaches Cloudfire.
6. Catherine later reaches Cloudfire and the *Restoration of Erathia* opening CG begins from its aftermath.

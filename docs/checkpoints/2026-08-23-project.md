## Four-school identities

### Broader school direction

- **Decision:** Fire, Water, Air, and Earth should feel like substantially different weapons rather than one moveset with recoloured elemental effects.
- **Decision:** Distinct character motion should make the schools recognizable even without VFX.
- **Decision:** Each school also uses distinct VFX for immediate switching readability.
- **Decision:** All schools use the same underlying combat timing; their animations and hitboxes create different perceived motion and feel.
- **Decision — perceived motion order:** Earth is slowest, Fire is medium, Water is faster and swifter, and Air is fastest.
- Exact animation staging, hitboxes, spatial identity, and presentation remain prototype-production work.

### Fire

- **Decision:** Fire focuses on stacking damage-over-time effects.
- **Prototype decision:** Fire `Y` applies multiple DoT stacks according to accumulated light attacks.
- No additional Fire scaling behavior is required for the first test; exact values remain open.

### Water

- **Decision:** Water’s broader identity includes crowd control, healing, and icy moves.
- **Prototype decision:** Water `Y` deals damage and applies **Frozen**.
- **Decision:** Frozen means fully incapacitated: the enemy cannot move or attack.
- **Prototype decision:** accumulated light attacks increase Water `Y`’s affected area.
- **Superseded:** the earlier prototype version in which Water applied Slow.
- **Deferred candidates:** Wet/Slow/Frozen progression, linear duration or strength scaling, and other area-scaling variants.

### Air

- **Decision:** Air is swift and lightning-based; lightning may concentrate on one target or chain across several.
- **Prototype decision:** Air `XY` applies an attack-speed buff based on Laema’s current attack speed.
- The illustrative `10%` value is not authoritative.
- **Decision:** the buff has a duration and persists across school switches; its exact duration and percentage remain open.
- **Prototype decision:** Air `XXY` through `XXXXY` releases chain lightning whose damage and chained-target count scale with accumulated light attacks.

### Earth

- **Decision:** Earth is stable, slow, powerful, defensive, and deliberately cumbersome.
- **Decision:** Earth deals greater damage per hit and uses the reference feel of *Monster Hunter* Great Sword and Hammer.
- **Decision:** Earth has small to no hit reaction as part of its style.
- **Prototype decision:** Earth `Y` applies Slow.
- Accumulated light attacks increase the Slow’s affected area, slowing percentage, and duration; exact values remain open.

## Prototype combo and school-switching grammar

- **Prototype decision:** `X` is light attack and `Y` is heavy attack.
- **Prototype decision:** the basic sequence family is `XY`, `XXY`, `XXXY`, and `XXXXY`.
- **Decision:** school switching may occur during an ongoing combo and does not reset the accumulated combo count.
- **Decision:** a combo may use no more than two schools in the prototype.
- **Prototype controls:** D-pad Up selects Fire, Down Water, Left Air, and Right Earth.
- **Decision:** every attack animation contains a window that reads the next combo input.
- **Decision:** a D-pad school switch may be entered during that window as part of the combo sequence.
- **Decision:** switching consumes neither an attack opportunity nor a combo level; the following `X` or `Y` continues the chain.
- **Decision:** if the input window closes without a valid next attack, the accumulated combo level resets.
- **Decision:** when attack animation speed increases, its timeline-based input window shortens proportionally with it.
- **Open:** how an attack input pressed before its reading window opens is handled.

### Prototype-only layered finisher

- **Prototype-only decision:** the school performing the final `Y` resolves its specialty at the full accumulated combo level.
- The previously used school also resolves its specialty two levels lower.
- Example: `Fire XX → Earth XXY` resolves Earth at level 5 and Fire at level 3.
- This rule exists only to test whether two-school mixed finishers are fun; it is not a final-game contract.

## Defence

The user verified and locked the current blocking overview:

- **Decision:** Water and Earth use sustained blocking.
- **Decision:** Fire and Air use parrying rather than sustained blocking.
- **Decision:** blocking prevents incoming damage while the guard remains intact.
- **Decision:** holding block drains the combo counter.
- **Decision:** prolonged blocking is the mistake punished by guard break.
- **Decision:** VFX and SFX warn when guard break is approaching.
- **Decision:** parrying prevents damage only during its active timing window and currently gives no additional reward.
- **Decision:** perfect block is not currently a separate mechanic.

Still open:

- whether guard-break buildup uses hit count or blocked damage;
- exact thresholds, drain rates, and parry timing;
- what mechanically happens when guard breaks;
- whether defence is stationary and aimed with the right stick.

## Acceleration and momentum

- **Decision — target experience:** combat acceleration should create flow or an “in the zone” feeling in which the player tries to keep up with the pace.
- **Proposal:** completed combos add their attack count to a cumulative counter; milestones shorten attack-animation duration.
- All numeric examples, thresholds, BPM values, percentages, limits, and curves are illustrative only.
- **Proposal:** taking damage removes momentum substantially faster than momentum is built.
- Successful defence preserving or slightly increasing momentum was discussed but deliberately removed from the locked Blocking Overview; its relationship to momentum therefore remains open.
- **Constraint:** any BPM or rhythmic-subdivision language must not create an external-beat rhythm-game rule.

## Panel feedback (verbatim)

```text
Highlights

The pitch proposed a fresh and unusual gameplay toy rather than simply recombining familiar game structures.
The central idea introduced an interesting tension between remaining protected and deliberately exposing oneself to perform stronger actions.
The proposal suggested potential for different combat styles, timing patterns, and risk/reward decisions.
The attempt to explore a new form of interaction was interesting and worth presenting.

Areas for Improvement

The proposal raised many questions about how the toy would work in practice, how enjoyable it would feel, and whether it could sustain player interest over time.
The presentation focused heavily on low-level technical rules and details but did not provide enough evidence of the broader player experience those rules would create.
Timing windows, input requirements, reset conditions, and the relationship between the different parts of the system were difficult to understand.
Most importantly, the judge panel was not convinced that the proposed toy was strong, deep, or versatile enough to support the development of a complete game around it.
The pitch did not demonstrate clearly how the central interaction could evolve across several hours through new situations, challenges, player decisions, or forms of mastery.

Potential Iteration Directions

Prototype the central input loop with minimal surrounding systems and let people play it.
Demonstrate progression through three increasingly demanding combat situations rather than explaining the highest-complexity version first.
Make the risk/reward legible: clearly show what the player gains by exposing themselves and what they risk when the timing goes wrong.
Present the experience first from the player's perspective: what the player sees, decides, performs, risks, and gains. Introduce low-level technical details only when they help explain that experience.
Demonstrate the toy across meaningfully different gameplay situations to show that it can evolve rather than relying on the same interaction repeatedly.
Clarify what forms of mastery, variation, and progression could allow the toy to sustain a complete game.
Use the next iteration to answer the central question directly: why is this toy strong enough to build a complete game around?
```
